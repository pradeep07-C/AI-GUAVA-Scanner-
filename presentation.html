Slide 2 / 12 • Background & Problem The Post-Harvest Challenge

Problem Statement & Motivation

  • • High Perishability: Guava (Psidium guajava) is a climacteric fruit subject to sudden respiration spikes and rapid pericarp softening.
  • • Heavy Supply Chain Losses: 25% to 40% of post-harvest yield is lost during transport, storage, and market handling.
  • • Public Health Risks: Rotting fruits harbor fungal pathogens like Colletotrichum that release toxic mycotoxins, causing acute food poisoning.
  • • Manual Sorting Bottlenecks: Manual inspection by hand is slow, inconsistent, subjective, and prone to human error.
🗣️ Presenter Script: "Guavas ripen and degrade very rapidly after harvest. In traditional supply chains, over a quarter of the harvest is lost to decay. Manual sorting done by hand is subjective and inconsistent. More importantly, consuming spoiled guavas is hazardous because fungal pathogens produce mycotoxins that cause food poisoning. This created the need for an automated scanner."
Slide 3 / 12 • Objectives & Scope System Goals

Project Objectives & Deliverables

1. Autonomous Grading

Classify guava images into 3 commercial freshness grades with exact consumer messages:

  • Fresh: "it is fresh you can eat"
  • Medium: "it is also eatable but freshness is less"
  • Poor: "poor condition - not recommended for eating"
2. Pathology Diagnostics

Identify the specific disease agent (Anthracnose, Stylar End Rot, Fruit Fly) and issue explicit dietary warnings.

3. Out-of-Distribution Rejection

Reject non-dataset images (cars, animals, arbitrary photos) with the exact message: "cant recognized".

4. Information Encapsulation

Keep internal server paths (e.g. medium/guava_050.jpg) hidden from end-user cards to ensure clean presentation.

Slide 10 / 12 • Pathology Diagnostics Disease Analysis

Guava Diseases & Health Warnings

1. Guava Anthracnose (Colletotrichum gloeosporioides)

Circular, sunken dark necrotic lesions with concentric spore rings developed under humid post-harvest conditions.

2. Stylar End Rot (Phomopsis psidii)

Water-soaked brown decay originating at the floral blossom calyx and progressing along the fruit axis.

3. Fruit Fly Infestation (Bactrocera dorsalis)

Puncture oviposition scars followed by internal maggot tunneling and secondary bacterial soft rot.

4. Guava Wilt (Fusarium oxysporum)

Vascular xylem occlusion causing small, shriveled, desiccated fruits with dry internal pulp.

⚠️ Health Advisory: Fungal mycelium produces mycotoxins that trigger nausea and acute gastrointestinal irritation. Damaged fruits must be discarded safely.
🗣️ Presenter Script: "When a fruit falls into the Poor category, our system provides an intelligent pathology diagnosis. It also generates a clear health advisory explaining the biological cause and warning consumers that rotting pulp harbors mycotoxins that can cause digestive illness."
